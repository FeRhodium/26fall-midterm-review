Depth-first search
• Depth-first search is a surprisingly versatile linear-time procedure that
  reveals a wealth of information about a graph.
• The most basic question it addresses:
   • What parts of the graph are reachable from a given vertex?
• Exploring a graph = like exploring a maze.
   • From current vertex, move to another.
   • Until you get stuck.
   • Then backtrack till you find a new place to explore.
   • You may use the “left-hand” rule.

                   Non-recursive definition of BFS/DFS
                   • BFS and DFS are similar except we use a
                     queue for BFS and a stack for DFS (to
                     store the new unexplored nodes).
                   • BFS: the nodes are explored in sequence
                     they are discovered: FIFO property of
                     queue.
                   • DFS: the nodes are explored in the
                     reverse sequence they are discovered:
                     LIFO property of stack.

Conclusions
• DFS is a graph search that generates a set of DFS trees.
• It does more than just reachability by characterizing edges of the
  graph.
   • To characterize edges, it keeps timing information generated during
     the search.
• Useful to solve problems like cycle detection, topological sort in
  DAGs, finding strong components in graphs (not examined here),
  etc.
• DFS can be done as a “dual” of BFS if we use a stack instead of a
  queue for storing newly discovered nodes.
