Motivation
• Very general and abstract model: n balls, m bins.
• Random choice of bin for each ball.
• Study the stochastic properties of the bin occupancy process.
• Applications: hashing, scheduling, …
• First, we introduce some concentration bounds:
    • If we know the mean of a r.v. 𝐸[𝑋], how to estimate the probability
      that 𝑋 takes a larger value.
    • In many cases we like to show that this can happen very infrequently.
    • There are ways to get upper bounds for this probability. If these are
      small enough, then we know that the actual probability is also small.

Concentration bounds
• Bound the probability of a random variable to be far from its mean.
• Use very simple information about the random variable.
   • We only know the mean: μ = E[X] : Markov inequality.
   • We know the mean μ = E[X] and the variance Ε[(X-μ)2] : Chebyshev's inequality.
   • If X is the sum of iid bounded variables: Chernoff and Hoeffding’s bounds.

Balls and bins
• Very general model: we throw 𝑛 balls to 𝑚 bins
• Questions: average number of balls in a bin, average number of empty bins, average
  number of collisions, how many balls on the average we need to throw to have all bins full,
  average number of balls in the most loaded bin,…
• Relation to hashing: we assume that the hash function distributes the 𝑛 keys (balls)
  randomly over the 𝑚 locations (bins) of the hash table (SUHA).
• Relation to job scheduling: we have 𝑛 jobs and 𝑚 machines and must assign jobs (balls) to
  these machines (bins). Assume 𝑚 = 𝑛
    • Case of no information: randomly assign jobs to machines using a hash function.
      Worse load of a machine = log 𝑛 / log log 𝑛 whp.
    • Case of “little” information: The “power of two choice” phenomenon: for each job,
      choose randomly two machines (i.e., use 2 hash functions) and assign the job to the
      less loaded machine between the two. Then worse load = log log 𝑛 / log log log 𝑛 whp.
