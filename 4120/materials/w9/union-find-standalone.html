Islands and Trees

Twelve towns, eighteen cables arriving one at a time. For each cable we must decide fast: are its two ends already on the same island? Read the panel below, then step through. If you are following a chat session, answer there first and press Next afterwards.

Before you start

The story

Twelve towns, A to L, start with no cables. Cables arrive one at a time, in a fixed list. Towns joined by arrived cables form an island: you can travel between any two towns of an island, and between towns of different islands you cannot. Each arriving cable either joins two islands into a bigger one, or links two towns that are already on the same island and so adds nothing.

A computer must answer one question again and again: are these two towns on the same island? It does so by representing every island as a tree. Each town stores an arrow to its parent. The town at the top, the root, has no parent and gives the island its name. To find the island of a town, follow the arrows up to the root.

The goal of this page. A find is only as fast as the number of arrows it follows, so we want trees that stay short. In the lecture you will prove that, with the rank rule below, no find ever follows more than log2 n arrows. This page does not prove that. It lets you see, on one example, the facts that the proof uses: how the rank rule works, why a town is never deeper than its root's rank, and how few towns a tree of a given rank can have. At the very end there is one extra move, path compression, that makes the cost per operation almost constant.

Words used here

  • Town: a circle with a letter. Cable: a line joining two towns.
  • Island: a set of towns joined by the cables that have arrived. Its colour on the map is just a help to the eye.
  • Tree, parent, root: an island stored as arrows pointing up from each town to its parent. The root names the island.
  • makeset(x): create a new island with the single town x. find(x): follow the arrows from x up to the root. union: join two islands by putting one root under the other.
  • Rank (shown as r0, r1, ...): a number stored with each town, starting at 0. The rank of a root says roughly how tall its tree can be. A town’s rank never changes once the town has a parent.
  • Depth of a town: the number of arrows from it up to its root. A root has depth 0.
  • Path compression: after a find, point every town on the path walked straight at the root.
  • log* n: how many times you can take log2, starting from n, before the value is 1 or less. It grows extremely slowly.

The tabs choose the method

  • Rank rule: join two islands by putting the root of smaller rank under the root of larger rank. On a tie the second root goes under the first, and the new root’s rank goes up by 1.
  • Path compression: take this last. The same cables and the same rule, with one extra move after every find. It starts again from the beginning.
  • Take them left to right, or follow your chat session. Each tab keeps its own place.
  • The three counters above the map show the number of islands, the deepest town and the highest rank.

Why the cost is almost constant

A long find flattens the path it walked, so later finds from those towns take 1 arrow. And because of the rank rule, no tree can ever be deeper than log n, so long finds stay rare. Over many operations the average cost per operation is about log* n. This is how small that is:
