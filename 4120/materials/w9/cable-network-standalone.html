Cheapest Cable Network

One network to build, six cities, nine possible cables. Read the story below, then step through each method. If you are following a chat session, answer there first and press Next afterwards.

Before you start

The story

Six cities want to be joined into one network. Nine cables could be laid, and the drawing below shows which two cities each cable would join. The number on a cable is its weight: what it costs to lay.

The network must let every city reach every other city, directly or through other cities, and we want to pay as little as possible. A cable between two cities that are already connected adds nothing and only closes a loop, so it is wasted. The tabs below try different ways of choosing which cables to lay.

Words used here

  • City: a circle with a letter, A to F.
  • Cable: a line joining two cities. Its number is its weight.
  • Kept: a cable we lay. Skipped: a cable we decide not to lay. Removed: a cable laid and then taken out again.
  • Loop: a ring of cables that comes back to the city it started from.
  • Cut: a split of the cities into two groups. The cables across the cut join a city in one group to a city in the other.
  • Allowed cut: a cut that no kept cable crosses, meaning no kept cable has one end in each group.
  • Spanning tree: cables that connect all the cities with no loop. Six cities need five cables.
  • Total weight: the weights of the kept cables added up.

The tabs choose the method

  • Any order: go down a list of cables in the given order. Lay a cable unless its two cities are already connected.
  • Cheapest first: the same method with the list sorted from the lightest cable to the heaviest.
  • Repair loops: lay every cable. When a loop appears, take out the heaviest cable on it.
  • Cut method: repeatedly choose an allowed cut and keep the lightest cable across it.
  • Take them from left to right, or follow your chat session. Each tab keeps its own place.

The buttons move through it

  • Next shows the next cable: whether it is kept, skipped or removed, and the total weight so far. At the end of a method it moves on to the next tab.
  • Back goes one step back. Restart returns the current tab to its first step.
  • The Earlier steps strip at the bottom keeps a small picture of every step you have seen. Click one to jump back to it.
  • On the Cut method tab, the two buttons in Check your answer reveal the lightest cable across the cut (and keep it) and compare with the tree from the cheapest-first run.
  • The ← and → keys also step back and forward.
