How to handle simultaneous mix of different types of apps?

  • Assumptions encoded into many schedulers:
     • Apps that sleep a lot and have short bursts must be interactive
       apps – they should get high priority
     • Apps that compute a lot should get low(er?) priority, since they

              Case study: Linux O(1) scheduler

• Heuristics
   • User-task priority adjusted ±5 based on heuristics
      • P → sleep_avg = sleep_time – run_time
      • Higher sleep_avg ⇒ more I/O bound the task, more reward (and vice
        versa)
   • Interactive Credit (IC)
      • Earned when a task sleeps for a “long” time
      • Spend when a task runs for a “long” time
      • IC is used to provide hysteresis to avoid changing interactivity for
        temporary changes in behavior
   • However, “interactive tasks” get special dispensation
      • To try to maintain interactivity
      • Placed back into active queue, unless some other task has been
        starved for too long…

                   Why max-min fairness?

• Weighted Fair Sharing / Proportional Shares
   • User 1 gets weight 2, user 2 weight 1
• Priorities
   • Give user 1 weight 1000, user 2 weight 1
• Reservations
   • Ensure user 1 gets 10% of a resource
   • Give user 1 weight 10, sum weights ≤ 100
• Deadline-based scheduling
   • Given a user job’s demand and deadline, compute user’s
     reservation/weight
• Isolation
   • Users cannot affect others beyond their share

                    Solution: virtual time

• Key observation: while the finish times of packets may change
  when a new packet arrives…
   • The order in which packets finish doesn’t!
   • Only the order is important for scheduling
• Solution: instead of the packet finish time maintain the number
  of rounds needed to send the remaining bits of the packet
  (virtual finishing time)
    • Virtual finishing time doesn’t change when the packet arrives
• System virtual time – index of the round in the bit-by-bit round
  robin scheme

                             Conclusion

• Max-min fairness
   • Each job gets its “fair share”
   • Provides isolation: a job is guaranteed its fair share
• Weighted fair sharing:
   • A way to provide priorities: give higher weights to higher priority jobs
   • Avoid starvation: as long as a job has a non-negative weight it will
     get service
• Various implementations trading between complexity, accuracy,
  efficiency:
   • Fair sharing, Lottery scheduling, EEVDF
